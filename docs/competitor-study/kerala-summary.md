# Kerala Competitor Landscape — Summary

**Source file:** `docs/competitor-study/kerala-competitors.xlsx` (sheet: "Kerala Competitors")
**Compiled:** 2026-07-28 · **Method:** live web search (Google/Firecrawl search) against wedding directories (WedMeGood, WeddingWire, JustDial), Instagram, and company sites — every row has a verified real URL. No companies were invented.

## Totals

- **30 companies** found and verified.
- **18** are Kerala-headquartered or Kerala-focused (wedding/event planners and one photography marketplace).
- **12** are pan-India platforms that actively serve the Kerala market (marketplaces, SaaS tools, invitation makers, one registration/ticketing platform).

## Notable patterns

1. **The Kerala market is planner-heavy, tech-light.** Of the 18 Kerala-based companies, 15–16 are pure offline/services businesses (decor, coordination, vendor booking) with a brochure website and Instagram — no guest list, RSVP, invitation, or budgeting tool of their own. Digital capability in Kerala is currently borrowed from national marketplaces (WedMeGood, WeddingWire) rather than built in-house.
2. **Only one Kerala-headquartered full-suite competitor exists: itsmy.wedding** (Trivandrum, founded 2025). It is the single most direct competitor to Evenzi's feature set — AI checklist, budget manager, guest list + RSVP, e-invitations, and inspiration/media galleries, plus a vendor marketplace layer Evenzi does not have. Worth close, ongoing monitoring.
3. **Digital invitations is the most commoditized sub-category** — DesiEvite, Rayon Invitations, InviteCrafter, Wedd.ai, and Bliss all compete narrowly here, several with Kerala/Malayalam-specific template lines. Pricing where published is low and per-invite (₹99–₹399), suggesting a race-to-the-bottom, high-volume model rather than a subscription/SaaS one.
4. **Guest list + RSVP + WhatsApp is the emerging wedge.** Bliss and Wedd.ai are both narrowly built around "replace the WhatsApp group and Excel sheet with a link + WhatsApp nudges" — this is the clearest open lane for Evenzi to differentiate with a broader, bundled product rather than a point solution.
5. **Pricing is mostly opaque.** Only 6 of 30 companies publish any concrete price (Panigrahana ~₹2L planning fee, SANS ₹30K, Expressario ~₹50K/day, Bliss ₹99/party invite, SitesPlaced ₹999 one-time, DesiEvite ₹399/video invite). The rest — including every pure services planner — quote on request. Sales outreach should expect a discovery call to be required for pricing on ~80% of this list.
6. **No pure Kerala-only SaaS website builder or media-gallery product was found.** Click Me Happy (photography booking with per-shoot online galleries) is the closest Kerala-native media-gallery play, but it is a photographer marketplace, not a couple-facing gallery product like Evenzi's planned Media & Memories feature — a potential gap Evenzi's roadmap already targets.
7. **Corporate-only players exist alongside wedding players** (The Event People, Cevex, and to a lesser extent Vibgyor/Angels/Melodia do both) — useful context if Evenzi ever considers a corporate-events adjacent product, but out of scope for the current wedding-first MVP.

## Coverage gaps / caveats for the sales team

- Several Kerala planners (Kalamaya Event, Gaala Wedding Planners, SitesPlaced Celebrations, DesiEvite, InviteCrafter) had **no discoverable social handle** — outreach for these should go through the website contact form or listed phone/WhatsApp number instead of social DM.
- A few social handles (marked "unverified handle" in the sheet) were inferred from the company name pattern but not directly confirmed on-platform — spot-check before using in an outreach sequence.
- ShaadiSaga's own site blocked automated fetch (bot protection); its reality and business details were cross-verified via LinkedIn, Crunchbase, and its acquisition writeup by Matrimony.com instead.
- This list skews toward Kochi/Ernakulam and Thrissur; Kozhikode, Trivandrum and Kollam are present but thinner — a second pass specifically targeting those cities (plus Kannur/Palakkad) would likely surface more regional planners if deeper Kerala-only coverage is wanted.
