## 2. Target Audience

### Primary: The Indian Host

**Who they are:**
The primary Evenzi user is an Indian family member — usually a parent, bride or groom, or close sibling — who has taken responsibility for organizing a wedding or major celebration. They are based in an urban or semi-urban area in India, aged 24–55, and comfortable using smartphones and WhatsApp daily. They may or may not have prior experience with digital planning tools.

**Demographic profile:**
- Age: 24–55 (core: 28–45)
- Location: Tier 1 and Tier 2 Indian cities (Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, Kochi, Jaipur, Chandigarh)
- Income: Middle to upper-middle class
- Device: Primarily mobile (Android and iOS), some desktop for detailed planning
- Tech comfort: High with WhatsApp and social apps, moderate with SaaS tools

**What they stress about:**
- Losing track of who confirmed attendance vs. who hasn't responded
- Managing different sub-events (Mehendi, Sangeet, Reception) with overlapping guest lists
- Coordinating between two families with different communication preferences
- Forgetting to follow up with non-respondents
- Not having a single place where all the information lives

**What they want:**
- To feel in control without being buried in spreadsheets
- To communicate with guests without sending 200 individual WhatsApp messages manually
- To share the joy — photos, moments, updates — in a way that feels personal
- To finish the planning feeling like the event went smoothly, not like they survived it

**Quote that captures their mindset:**
*"I just want one place where I can see who's coming and who isn't, and not have to dig through my chats for it."*

---

### Secondary: The Corporate Event Organizer

**Who they are:**
An office admin, HR professional, or operations coordinator responsible for company events — annual parties, team outings, Diwali gatherings, farewell events. They use Evenzi because it handles the logistics of guest management, RSVPs, and invitations without requiring enterprise software.

**Key difference from primary audience:**
Less emotional investment in the event itself, more focus on efficiency and avoiding headaches. Motivated by "getting it done cleanly."

**Note for MVP:** This audience is served by the same core features as the primary audience. No separate product track is needed. Marketing can acknowledge them without being built around them.

## 3. The Problem We Solve

### How Indian Families Plan Events Today

The current state of Indian event planning looks like this:

- **WhatsApp groups:** One for the "main family group," one for "close friends only," one for "bride's side," one for "groom's side." Information scattered across all of them.
- **Excel or Google Sheets:** A guest list someone started two months ago that has three versions, two of which are outdated.
- **Paper and memory:** Sub-event guest lists written on paper. Dietary preferences noted in a saved WhatsApp message from six months ago.
- **Manual follow-up:** Calling or messaging every non-respondent individually. No visibility into who has and hasn't responded.
- **Photo sharing:** Seven different albums in different WhatsApp chats, phone galleries, and Google Photos links.

### The Pain

**Coordination chaos:** Information lives in too many places, owned by too many people. When you want to know who is confirmed for the Mehendi, you have to ask three different people and reconcile three different answers.

**Manual tracking:** RSVPs are tracked by memory, re-asking the same people, or a spreadsheet that someone has to remember to update. Missed follow-ups are guaranteed.

**The human cost:** This is not a productivity problem. A bride's mother managing all this coordination cannot fully enjoy her daughter's celebrations. A groom tracking his own guest list on the morning of his wedding is not where he should be spending mental energy.

**The emotional stakes:** This is someone's wedding. Or their father's 60th birthday. Or the family's first big celebration after years apart. These events matter deeply. The stress that bad tooling adds to them is real and unnecessary.

## 4. Our Solution

### One Platform — Less Chaos

Evenzi brings the entire planning process into one place. Guest list, sub-events, RSVPs, invitations, media — all connected, all visible, all in one dashboard. The host always knows where things stand.

### WhatsApp-Native — No Behavior Change Required

Indian users live in WhatsApp. Evenzi meets them there. Invitations go out via WhatsApp. RSVP links are shared in WhatsApp. Hosts do not need to convince their relatives to download a new app or learn a new system. The familiar tool carries the new capability.

### Free to Start — No Barrier to Try

The biggest obstacle to adopting a new tool is friction at the entry point. Evenzi removes it. No credit card. No lengthy setup. Create an account, create your event, start inviting guests. The value is immediate.

### Built for Indian Celebrations Specifically

Not "event planning software" with Indian languages added. Sub-events are named Mehendi, Sangeet, Haldi — not "pre-event 1." RSVP communications are designed around Indian social dynamics. Phone OTP works with every Indian mobile number from day one. The product understands the context.

## 5. Competitive Landscape

### WeddingWire India / The Knot India

**What they do:** Wedding vendor directories. Couples use them to find and compare photographers, caterers, venue decorators.

**Overlap with Evenzi:** Minimal. These platforms are for vendor discovery, not event management. They help you find your caterer — they do not help you track whether your caterer has confirmed the menu with you.

**Evenzi's edge:** Evenzi serves the host side of event planning — coordination, guests, RSVPs, memories — which these platforms do not touch.

---

### Shaadi.com

**What they do:** Matrimonial matchmaking platform — connecting families for marriage proposals.

**Overlap with Evenzi:** None. Shaadi.com is about finding a partner. Evenzi is about planning the wedding after you have.

**Why we mention it:** Users sometimes conflate "wedding platform" broadly. Evenzi is the planning tool for after the match is made.

---

### Manual Tools: WhatsApp + Excel

**What they are:** The real incumbent. Not a company — a behavior. The combination of WhatsApp group chats and a shared spreadsheet is what nearly every Indian family uses today.

**Why this is the hardest competitor:**
- Zero switching cost — everyone already uses WhatsApp
- No learning curve — they already know how to use it
- Familiar — they've planned events this way before and it "worked"
- No price — it's free

**Why Evenzi wins:**
- WhatsApp + Excel has no RSVP tracking — just asking people again and again
- It has no guest visibility dashboard — no one person knows the complete picture
- It has no sub-event management — you build separate sheets and separate groups for each ceremony
- It has no media organization — photos scatter across chats and galleries
- It has no delegation — you cannot give a family member controlled access to part of the planning

The key insight: WhatsApp + Excel does not feel like a problem until you are in the middle of it. The marketing job is to make people recognize the pain before they are drowning in it.

## 9. Launch Messaging Strategy

### Phase 1: Awareness in Target Market (MVP Launch)

**Target:** Engaged couples and their immediate families in urban India. People who have a wedding to plan in the next 6–18 months.

**Channel priority:**

| Channel | Role | Approach |
|---------|------|---------|
| Instagram | Primary awareness | Feature showcases, real event stories, tips content |
| WhatsApp | Primary conversion | Word-of-mouth, referral links, direct sharing |
| Wedding planner partnerships | High-leverage acquisition | Partner with 5–10 local wedding planners who recommend Evenzi to their clients |
| Influencer / creator | Secondary | 1–2 Indian wedding content creators, micro-influencer tier |

**Initial hook:** "Your wedding planning, sorted. Free."

**Core CTA:** "Create your event free at evenzi.com" — one action, no friction.

### Phase 2: Growth Through Organic Sharing

The product itself is the growth engine. When a host sends invitations to 200 guests via Evenzi, each of those guests sees the Evenzi-powered RSVP page. Guests become future hosts.

**Referral mechanic (to be built):** When a guest RSVPs through Evenzi and later creates their own event, track the referral. Reward referring hosts when paid tiers launch.

### Phase 3: Vendor Marketplace (Post-MVP)

Once the host base is established, open the platform to vendor sign-ups. The marketplace creates a two-sided network effect: more hosts attract more vendors, more vendors attract more hosts.

This phase changes the marketing message:
- For hosts: "Find and book the best vendors for your celebration"
- For vendors: "Get discovered by thousands of Indian families planning their celebrations"
